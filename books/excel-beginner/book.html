What Excel Is For

Rows and columns of boxes that add up your numbers.

Let's say you've typed a month of expenses into a column and need the total. Excel is made of rows and columns of boxes called cells. Each cell can hold a number, some text or a formula.

Once your data is in rows and columns, Excel can add it up, sort and filter it, put it in tables, and build charts. It's good for simple sums and for tracking almost any kind of information.

A total you type in yourself stays put when the numbers under it change.

Put the numbers in cells and let Excel do the adding.

Workbooks, Sheets and Cells

The file, its pages, and the name of every box on them.

Let's say someone asks you for the sales file. That file is a workbook, the Excel file you save. A workbook holds sheets, and you can add as many sheets as you want to one workbook.

Each cell has an address made from its column letter and row number, such as B2. Without addresses, you'd have to describe every box as "the box in the third column of the second row" each time you wanted to point at one.

The workbook is the file, the sheet is a page inside it, and B2 names one box.

Get from the top of a list to the bottom without the mouse.

A number that looks right but won't add up.
