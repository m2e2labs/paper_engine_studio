Overview

Power Query Is Not DAX, and Not Excel

Two languages, two moments. Excel is neither.

Let's say your Sales workbooks each arrive with a junk title row, and you're hunting for a formula to fix it. By the time DAX runs, the data's already in the model.

Power Query gets the data in and shapes it, in M, while it loads. DAX works on the model once it's loaded, and a measure runs when someone looks. It points at whole columns, never cells.

Fix the shape in Power Query. Ask the questions in DAX.

DO THIS NOW

Open your own file and look at Applied Steps. Everything in that list ran before a single chart did.

Applied Steps: A Recipe, Not an Edit

Every click is a step. The steps are the query.

Let's say you've removed a junk row and fixed a type, and it feels like you edited the file. You didn't.

Every click became a step, and the list is the query. The steps run top to bottom, each working on what the last one handed it. Click one to see the data right there. Rename, reorder, delete: the source is never touched.

You're not editing the data. You're writing a recipe for it.

Every query loads, unless you say otherwise.

Build as many as you like. Load what you'll use.

The icon in every header decides what comes next.

Promote Headers and Remove the Junk Rows

Take the junk off the top first. Then promote.

Let's say your Sales sheet has a title row above the headers. Automatic detection adds two steps by itself, promoting that title and setting types.

Delete those two steps. Then remove the top rows, leaving the real headers in row one, and promote them. The other way round, the title becomes your column name.

Junk off first, headers second.

Choosing, Removing and Renaming Columns

Name what you keep, not what you drop.

Let's say you removed Notes. Next month the source adds Channel, and Remove Columns lets it through: it only named what to drop.

Choose Columns names what to keep, so anything new stays out. It's Microsoft's answer for changing columns. Rename here too: the report shows these names.

Adding a column later is easy. Removing one isn't.

Replace Values, Trim and Clean

Two values that look the same, and aren't.

Let's say you group your sales by Region and get two Norths. One of them has a space in front of it.

The model trims trailing spaces but leaves leading ones, and those break relationships and skew visuals. Trim strips both ends, Clean takes out hidden control characters, and Replace Values swaps out a placeholder like N/A. Fix it before it loads.

Same letters, different value, until you Trim.

DO THIS NOW

Group by a text column of yours in Power Query. Two of the same thing means a hidden space, or different capitals.

Absolute Beginners · No. 12
Cleaning

Filtering Rows: Narrowing Without Deleting

The dropdown writes a step, and it runs every time.

Let's say you only want the North rows. You untick the rest in the column's dropdown, and that wrote a step. It's part of the recipe, so it runs again every refresh.

The tick list only loads the top 1,000 distinct values, so it isn't always everything. Filter early, and on a source that folds, the source does the filtering.

A filter isn't a view. It's a step.

Splitting One Column Into Many

Into columns, or into rows. Rows is the one people forget.

Let's say your Product column holds two things at once, like Bike/Lock. One cell, two facts.

Split Column by Delimiter cuts at the slash. Into columns gives you new columns named after the old one. Into rows keeps the same columns and adds rows, one product per cell. That's the one people forget, and often the one they need.

Two products in one cell is two rows waiting to happen.

Overview

Append or Merge: Which One You Need

More rows, or more columns. That's the whole decision.

Let's say January and February are separate tables, and each category sits on a Products sheet. Two different jobs.

Append adds one table's contents to another: same columns, more rows. Merge joins two tables on matching values, like Product: same rows, more columns.

Longer, append. Wider, merge.

DO THIS NOW

Say out loud: more rows, or more columns? That's the answer.

Third Watch PressCombining Queries

Combining page: append gathers the column headers of every table into one schema and lines the columns up by name, not by position, a column one table lacks shows nulls instead of an error, three or more tables can be appended in one step in the order they are selected, and Append queries as new leaves the original queries unchanged.

Absolute Beginners · No. 18
Combining

Append: Stacking Tables End to End

It lines columns up by name, not by position.

Let's say each month arrives as its own table. Append matches columns by name, not position, so February's odd column order still lines up.

But March says Area, not Region. No error. Just an extra column and nulls. And the Three or more tables option does every month in one step.

Append never complains. It just leaves a gap.

DO THIS NOW

Append two of your monthly tables, then rename a column in one of them and look for the nulls.

Third Watch PressCombining Queries

Combining page: a merge joins two tables on matching values, the first table picked is the left and the second the right, the join kind decides which rows survive, left outer keeping every left row, inner only the matches and left anti only the left rows with no match, and the merge adds one column named after the right table that then has to be expanded.

Absolute Beginners · No. 19
Combining

Merge: Joining Side by Side, and the Join Kinds

Match on a column, pick which rows survive, then expand.

Let's say your Sales rows name a product and the category lives on the Products sheet. A merge joins them on matching values in Product. The first table you pick is the left.

Left outer keeps every Sales row, inner only the matches, and left anti only the Sales rows with no match. Key columns can differ in name, not in data type.

The join kind decides which rows survive.

DO THIS NOW

Merge Sales with Products using a left anti join first, just to see which rows have no match. Then change it to left outer.

Third Watch PressCombining Queries

Combining page: duplicating a query makes a copy of it, while referencing makes a new query that uses the first query's steps without copying them, so any change to the original carries down, and Microsoft's guidance is to split a long query into smaller referenced queries, which right-clicking a step and choosing Extract Previous does for you.

Absolute Beginners · No. 20
Combining

Duplicate or Reference: Two Ways to Branch

A copy that drifts, or a pointer that follows.

Let's say you want a second query that starts from your cleaned Sales. Duplicate copies it, so fix Sales next week and the copy never hears.

Reference uses the steps of Sales without copying them, so any change carries down. To split a long query this way, right-click a step and choose Extract Previous.

Duplicate to go separate ways. Reference to share one fix.

DO THIS NOW

Reference your cleaned query, change a step in the original, watch both.

Absolute Beginners · No. 21
Performance

Query Folding: Letting the Source Do the Work

The source runs what it can, before the data leaves.

Let's say your sales live in a database, not a folder of workbooks. Query folding turns the steps it can into the database's own operations, and Power Query runs the rest.

In Microsoft's example, the query with no folding pulled over 3.6 million rows and took 6 minutes 1 second. Fully folded: 10 rows, 31 seconds. Excel and CSV files can't fold at all.

Same ten rows. One query pulled 3.6 million to find them.

Seeing Whether Your Query Folds

One right-click on a step, and one menu item to read.

Let's say a refresh has gone slow. Right-click a step and look for View Native Query. It shows the request Power Query sends to the source, so if it's there, it folded up to that step.

If it's greyed out on a source that normally offers it, a step stopped folding. The coloured folding icons in Microsoft's articles are Power Query Online only.

If you can see the query, the source is doing the work.

DO THIS NOW

Third Watch PressWhy It's Slow, and Why It Breaks

Performance page: some transformations, such as Capitalize each word, never fold, generally everything up to the last folding step folds and the rest runs afterwards, so the order of steps matters; Microsoft's guidance is to find the step that breaks folding and move later steps earlier, folding can start again once the column a non-folding step touched is removed, and the engine may reorder steps itself.

Absolute Beginners · No. 23
Performance

The Steps That Stop It Folding

Folding stops where the source has no way to do the step.

Let's say View Native Query went grey. Some steps can't run at the source: Capitalize each word never folds. Generally, steps after the last folding one run on your machine.

So order matters: move later steps above the one that breaks it. Remove the column the non-folding step touched and folding can restart. The engine may reorder steps too.

Do what the source can do first, and the rest last.

DO THIS NOW

Move a later step above the first grey one, and check again.

Refresh Errors: Renamed Columns and Moved Files

Let's say a refresh that worked last month stops dead. Someone renamed Region to Area, so the step naming Region finds nothing. The detail line names it.

A moved folder gives DataSource.NotFound. Fix the path: Transform data, Data source settings, Change Source.

Rename it at the source, and the step that named it breaks.

DO THIS NOW

When a refresh fails, read the detail line first. Then find that name.

A list of named steps, then the one you get back.

Parameters: One Place to Change the Path

A named value your queries read instead of a typed path.

Let's say the Sales folder moves to another drive, and its path is typed into several of your queries. That's one edit per query, and any you miss is still looking in the old place.

A parameter stores a single value so you can reuse it. Queries read its name instead of the path, so the path lives in one place, and changing its Current Value updates them straight away. Always set its type.

Type the path once. Let everything else read it.
