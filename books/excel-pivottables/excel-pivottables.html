PivotTables

Why PivotTables Exist

A PivotTable answers many questions about one list.

Let's say your sales list is finished. Someone asks for the total by region, so you write a SUMIF. Then they want it by salesperson, then by product. Every new question is another formula to write, check and keep right.

A PivotTable is an Excel tool that calculates, summarises and analyses a list, so you can see comparisons, patterns and trends. The first book called it a summary table. It's the same thing, and from here on this book uses its proper name.

One list can answer many questions.

Tidying a List Before You Pivot

Run a short checklist before you build.

Let's say a colleague sends you their sales list and you want to pivot it. You can't tell by looking whether it's in good shape.

Microsoft's advice comes down to a short checklist. Every column has a heading, and no two headings are the same. There's one row of headings, not two. No cells are merged. And no column mixes dates and text.

Tidy the list first, then build.

Inserting Your First PivotTable

Let's say your list is tidy and you're ready to build. It's natural to worry that building a PivotTable will change your list.

Reading Row Labels and Grand Totals

Know what each part of a finished PivotTable is.

Let's say your PivotTable shows a block of numbers. A number you can't place is a number you can't trust.

Row labels run down the left, column labels run across the top, and the numbers in the middle are your values.

Excel also adds subtotals and a Grand Total by itself, and you can show or hide them.

Labels tell you what, values tell you how much.

Excel's Recommended PivotTables

Excel can suggest a layout for you to start from.

Excel has a feature called Recommended PivotTables. It works out a layout for your data, which you can then rearrange.

Remove leading spaces from your data first, because they change the sort order.

Compact, Outline or Tabular

Choose how the table lays out its fields.

Let's say you're going to share the table. The default layout may not read like a normal sheet.

Compact form is the default. Tabular form gives one column for each field and room for field headings, and it's easy to copy to another worksheet. Outline form is like tabular but can show subtotals at the top of each group.

To change it, click in the PivotTable, then on the Design tab choose Report Layout in the Layout group.

Tabular reads most like a normal sheet.
