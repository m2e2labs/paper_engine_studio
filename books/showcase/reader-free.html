Cooking

Resting meat

Why you wait before you cut

Let's say you pull a steak off the pan and cut it right away. The juice runs out over the board, and what's left on your plate is dry.

Heat pushes the juice toward the middle of the meat. Give it a few minutes off the heat and it settles back through the whole piece. Same steak, nothing lost to the board.

The waiting is part of the cooking.

TRY THIS TONIGHT

Take it off the heat, put it on a warm plate, cover it loosely. Five minutes for a steak, fifteen for a roast. Then cut.

The bloom: fresh coffee grounds are full of carbon dioxide that pushes water away. A small first pour and a thirty second wait lets the gas escape so the rest of the water can actually reach the coffee.

The showcase
Coffee

The bloom

The first pour, and why it is small

Let's say your coffee keeps coming out thin and sour, and you've already tried grinding finer. Fresh grounds are full of gas, and gas pushes water away, so most of your pour runs past the coffee.

Wet them with a splash first, twice the weight of the coffee, and wait. The bed puffs into a dome and the bubbles stop. Now the water can get in.

Thirty seconds, and the rest of the pour finally works.

TRY THIS TOMORROW

Pour twice the coffee's weight in water, just enough to wet everything. Wait thirty seconds. Then pour the rest.

Progressive overload: your body adapts to exactly what you ask of it, so lifting the same weight every week keeps you the same. Adding a small amount each week is what makes you stronger.

The showcase
Fitness

Progressive overload

A little more than last week

Let's say you've been going to the gym for months, you never miss a session, and you look exactly the same. You've been lifting the same weight the whole time.

Your body only builds what it keeps being asked for. Ask for the same thing every week and it has no reason to change. Ask for slightly more, one extra rep or the smallest plate on the bar, and it has to adapt.

Small, boring, every week. That is the whole method.

THIS WEEK

Write down what you lifted. Next session add one rep, or the smallest weight you own. If you can't, keep it and try again next week.

Code

Circuit breaker

Stop calling the thing that's down

Let's say your app asks another service for something, maybe a payment check. One morning it's down, and your whole app goes slow, including the parts that don't use it.

Every request sits there waiting to time out. A breaker counts the failures, and after a few in a row it stops calling and answers straight away with whatever you chose to say instead. Now and then it tries one real call.

One thing broke. Not everything.

TELL THE AI

Put a circuit breaker around my calls to this service. Open it after a few failures, return a fallback right away, and retry one call now and then.
